Spaced repetition, briefly

Each word comes back just before you would have forgotten it. Answer well and the gap until you see it again grows; answer badly and it shrinks back to nothing. Words you find hard keep turning up, words you know get out of your way.

Grading yourself honestly

Say the definition out loud before you show it, then grade what actually happened: Again if you drew a blank, Hard if it came slowly, Good if it came, Easy if it was instant. The number under each button is when the word would come back. Grading yourself generously only buys you a word you will have forgotten by test day.

Used in context

Every card has a sentence using the word, written for this deck and not taken from any test question. Opening it before the definition caps that card at Hard — a word you needed help with is not yet a word you know, and the schedule should not pretend otherwise. Opening it after you have already checked yourself costs nothing.

Ten new words a day

New words arrive ten at a time, which is what stops all 230 from landing in one evening and coming due together forever after. Change the number under Progress.

Keep it on your phone

Added to your home screen it opens like an app: full screen, no address bar, one tap from wherever you keep it.

  • iPhone or iPad: open it in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. Apple's instructions
  • Android: open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen. Google's instructions

Your progress stays here

It lives in this browser, on this device. There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere. A different phone means different progress, and clearing your site data erases it — as does Safari's automatic purge for sites you have not opened in a week, which visiting it fixes.
